## 10-Mark Questions

---

### Q1. Explain grammar in professional writing with reference to tense, punctuation, and sentence structure.

**Why Grammar Matters in Professional Writing:**
Grammar is the foundation of clear, professional communication. Poor grammar leads to misunderstanding, reduces credibility, and makes the writer look unprofessional. In technical and scientific writing, accuracy in grammar is as important as accuracy in data.

---

**A. Tense Usage in Scientific Writing:**

Different sections of a research paper use different tenses:

| Section | Tense Used | Reason | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abstract | Past tense | Reports what was done | "The study examined..." |
| Introduction | Present tense | States known facts | "Machine learning is a branch of AI." |
| Literature Review | Past tense | Reports what others found | "Sharma (2022) found that..." |
| Methodology | Past tense | Describes what you did | "Data was collected using a survey." |
| Results | Past tense | Reports findings | "The accuracy was 92%." |
| Discussion | Present tense + Past tense | Interprets results (present) and refers to findings (past) | "The results suggest that..." / "The model achieved..." |
| Conclusion | Present tense | States general conclusions | "AI tools improve productivity." |

**Key rules:**
- Use **present tense** for general truths and accepted facts.
- Use **past tense** for completed actions and specific findings.
- Be **consistent** within a section — don't switch tenses randomly.

---

**B. Punctuation Rules in Technical Writing:**

| Punctuation | Rule | Correct Example | Incorrect Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Period (.) | End of a declarative sentence | The test was successful. | The test was successful |
| Comma (,) | Separates items in a list, after introductory phrases, before conjunctions in compound sentences | Python, Java, and C++ are popular. | Python Java and C++ are popular. |
| Semicolon (;) | Joins two related independent clauses | The test passed; the results were accurate. | The test passed, the results were accurate. (comma splice) |
| Colon (:) | Introduces a list or explanation | Three languages were used: Python, Java, and C++. | Three languages were used, Python, Java, and C++. |
| Apostrophe (') | Shows possession or contraction | The system's accuracy was high. | The systems accuracy was high. |
| Hyphen (-) | Joins compound modifiers before a noun | A well-designed algorithm | A well designed algorithm |
| Quotation marks ("") | For direct quotes or special terms | He said, "The test failed." | — |

**Key rules:**
- **Avoid comma splice** — don't join two independent sentences with just a comma.
- **Use Oxford comma** — "A, B, and C" (recommended in technical writing).
- **Do not use contractions** in formal writing — write "do not" instead of "don't."

---

**C. Sentence Structure:**

Good professional writing uses **clear, well-structured sentences**. Key principles:

1. **Keep sentences short and focused** — one idea per sentence.
   - ❌ "The system was designed using Python and it was tested on a large dataset and the results showed that it was effective and efficient."
   - ✅ "The system was designed using Python. It was tested on a large dataset. The results showed that it was both effective and efficient."

2. **Use parallel structure** — items in a list should follow the same grammatical pattern.
   - ❌ "The tool helps in writing, to edit, and checking grammar."
   - ✅ "The tool helps in writing, editing, and checking grammar."

3. **Avoid dangling modifiers** — make sure the modifier refers to the right subject.
   - ❌ "After running the test, the error was found." (Who ran the test?)
   - ✅ "After running the test, the team found the error."

4. **Subject-verb agreement** — singular subjects need singular verbs.
   - ❌ "The data shows that the results is accurate."
   - ✅ "The data show that the results are accurate."

### Q2. Discuss common errors in scientific writing with examples and corrections.

Scientific writing must be clear, precise, and grammatically correct. Below are the most common errors with examples and corrections.

**1. Tense Inconsistency:**
- ❌ "The experiment was conducted in 2023 and the results show improvement."
- ✅ "The experiment was conducted in 2023 and the results showed improvement."

**2. Subject-Verb Agreement:**
- ❌ "The results of the study shows a positive trend."
- ✅ "The results of the study show a positive trend."

**3. Comma Splice:**
- ❌ "The model performed well, it achieved 95% accuracy."
- ✅ "The model performed well. It achieved 95% accuracy." OR "The model performed well; it achieved 95% accuracy."

**4. Dangling Modifier:**
- ❌ "Using the new algorithm, the accuracy was improved."
- ✅ "Using the new algorithm, the researchers improved the accuracy."

**5. Wordiness / Redundancy:**
- ❌ "In order to achieve the purpose of improving the system..."
- ✅ "To improve the system..."

**6. Vague Pronoun Reference:**
- ❌ "The system was tested with the tool. It showed good results." (What showed good results — system or tool?)
- ✅ "The system was tested with the tool. The system showed good results."

**7. Incorrect Use of Articles:**
- ❌ "A accuracy of model was 90%."
- ✅ "The accuracy of the model was 90%."

**8. Run-on Sentence:**
- ❌ "The data was collected the analysis was performed the results were presented."
- ✅ "The data was collected. The analysis was performed. The results were presented."

**9. Incorrect Parallelism:**
- ❌ "The study aims to analyze data, identifying trends, and the writing of a report."
- ✅ "The study aims to analyze data, identify trends, and write a report."

**10. Using Informal Language:**
- ❌ "The system is pretty good and works great."
- ✅ "The system demonstrated satisfactory performance."

| Error Type | Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Tense inconsistency | "was conducted... show" | "was conducted... showed" |
| Subject-verb | "results shows" | "results show" |
| Comma splice | "performed well, it achieved" | "performed well; it achieved" |
| Dangling modifier | "Using X, the accuracy improved" | "Using X, the team improved accuracy" |
| Wordiness | "in order to" | "to" |
| Vague pronoun | "It showed results" | "The system showed results" |

### Q3. Explain the role, benefits, limitations, and ethical use of AI tools in writing and paraphrasing.

**What are AI Writing Tools?**
AI writing tools are software applications that use artificial intelligence to assist in writing, editing, paraphrasing, and grammar checking. Examples include ChatGPT, QuillBot, Grammarly, and Jasper.

**Role of AI Tools in Writing:**
1. **Grammar and spell checking** — tools like Grammarly detect errors in real-time.
2. **Paraphrasing** — tools like QuillBot rephrase sentences while keeping the meaning.
3. **Content generation** — ChatGPT can generate drafts, summaries, and outlines.
4. **Translation** — AI tools can translate text across languages.
5. **Citation assistance** — some tools help format references.
6. **Readability improvement** — AI can simplify complex sentences.

**Benefits of AI Tools:**
1. **Saves time** — faster writing and editing.
2. **Improves quality** — catches errors humans may miss.
3. **Helps non-native speakers** — improves grammar and fluency.
4. **Assists in brainstorming** — generates ideas and outlines.
5. **Consistent formatting** — ensures uniform style throughout.

**Limitations of AI Tools:**
1. **May produce inaccurate information** — AI can "hallucinate" facts.
2. **Lacks deep understanding** — cannot truly understand context like a human.
3. **Generic output** — AI-generated text may lack originality and depth.
4. **Over-reliance** — students may stop learning to write independently.
5. **Cannot replace critical thinking** — AI cannot analyze, evaluate, or argue effectively.
6. **Detection tools exist** — universities use AI detection software (Turnitin, GPTZero).

**Ethical Concerns:**
1. **Plagiarism** — submitting AI-generated text as your own work is academic dishonesty.
2. **Lack of attribution** — not disclosing AI use is unethical.
3. **Data privacy** — sensitive information entered into AI tools may be stored or used.
4. **Bias** — AI tools may reflect biases present in their training data.
5. **Academic integrity** — using AI to complete assignments undermines learning.

**Ethical Use Guidelines:**
- Use AI as a **tool, not a replacement** for your own thinking.
- Always **review and verify** AI-generated content.
- **Cite AI use** when required by your institution.
- Use AI for **learning and improving**, not just for completing tasks.
- Do **not copy-paste** AI output directly — rewrite in your own words.

## 5-Mark Questions

---

### Q1. Tense usage in scientific writing

Different sections of a paper use different tenses:

- **Present tense** — for general facts, established knowledge, and conclusions.
  - "Machine learning is widely used in data analysis."
- **Past tense** — for describing methods, reporting specific results, and reviewing literature.
  - "The survey was conducted among 200 students."
  - "Sharma (2022) found that..."
- **Present perfect** — for connecting past research to the current study.
  - "Several studies have examined this topic."

**Key rule:** Be consistent within each section. Do not mix tenses randomly.

| Section | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Present | "AI is transforming healthcare." |
| Literature Review | Past / Present Perfect | "Kumar (2021) studied..." / "Researchers have explored..." |
| Methodology | Past | "Data was collected using..." |
| Results | Past | "The accuracy was 94%." |
| Discussion | Present + Past | "The results suggest..." |
| Conclusion | Present | "The model is effective for..." |

---

### Q2. Punctuation rules in technical writing

Key punctuation rules for technical writing:

1. **Period (.)** — End every complete sentence with a period.
2. **Comma (,)** — Use after introductory phrases, between list items, and before conjunctions in compound sentences.
3. **Semicolon (;)** — Use to join two closely related sentences: "The test passed; the results were accurate."
4. **Colon (:)** — Use before a list: "Three tools were used: Grammarly, QuillBot, and ChatGPT."
5. **Apostrophe (')** — Use for possession: "the system's performance."
6. **Hyphen (-)** — Use in compound modifiers: "a well-designed system."
7. **Do not use contractions** — Write "do not" instead of "don't."
8. **Use Oxford comma** — "A, B, and C" (recommended in technical writing).

---

### Q3. Active voice vs Passive voice

| Feature | Active Voice | Passive Voice |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Subject + Verb + Object | Object + was/were + Verb (past participle) + by Subject |
| Example | "The team conducted the experiment." | "The experiment was conducted by the team." |
| Clarity | More direct and clear | Less direct |
| Use in technical writing | Preferred for clarity | Used in methodology sections |

**When to use each:**
- **Active voice** — preferred in most sections for clarity and directness.
  - "We analyzed the data." ✅
- **Passive voice** — used in methodology when the focus is on the action, not the doer.
  - "The data was analyzed using SPSS." ✅

**Key tip for exams:** Modern technical writing **prefers active voice** but passive voice is acceptable in methods sections.

### Q5. Technical and academic word usage

In technical writing, use **formal, precise words** instead of informal, vague ones:

| Informal / Vague ❌ | Formal / Precise ✅ |
|---|---|
| a lot of | numerous / substantial |
| big | significant / considerable |
| get | obtain / acquire |
| show | demonstrate / indicate |
| things | factors / elements / components |
| good | effective / satisfactory |
| bad | inadequate / unsatisfactory |
| pretty much | approximately |
| kind of | somewhat |
| find out | determine / investigate |

**Tips:**
- Avoid slang and colloquial expressions.
- Do not use contractions (don't → do not).
- Be specific — "34% improvement" is better than "significant improvement."

### Q6. AI tools such as ChatGPT and QuillBot

**ChatGPT:**
- Developed by OpenAI.
- Can generate text, answer questions, write essays, summarize, and brainstorm.
- Useful for drafting outlines, paraphrasing, and getting explanations.
- Limitation: may produce inaccurate or fabricated information ("hallucinations").

**QuillBot:**
- An AI paraphrasing tool.
- Rewrites sentences in different modes (Standard, Fluency, Formal, Creative, etc.).
- Useful for rephrasing sentences to avoid plagiarism.
- Limitation: output may still be too close to the original (patchwriting risk).

**Other tools:** Grammarly (grammar check), Turnitin (plagiarism detection), GPTZero (AI detection).

**Important:** These tools should be used as **assistants**, not as replacements for your own writing and thinking.

---

### Q7. Ethical concerns in AI paraphrasing

1. **Plagiarism risk** — If AI paraphrasing produces text too similar to the original, it is still plagiarism.
2. **Academic dishonesty** — Submitting AI-paraphrased text without disclosure violates academic integrity policies.
3. **Loss of learning** — Students who rely on AI don't develop their own writing skills.
4. **No attribution** — Not disclosing AI use is unethical, especially in academic submissions.
5. **Quality concerns** — AI may change the meaning while paraphrasing, leading to inaccurate content.
6. **Detection** — Universities increasingly use AI detection tools; getting caught has serious consequences.

**Ethical approach:**
- Use AI to **learn and improve**, not to bypass effort.
- Always **review** AI output for accuracy.
- **Disclose** AI use when required.
- **Rewrite** in your own words — don't just copy AI output.
